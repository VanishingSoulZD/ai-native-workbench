## 1. Research Objective

### 1.1 Real-world objective

This research supports a practical decision:

> **When facing a concrete knowledge-work task, be able to determine what class of AI to use, which product is appropriate, and how the work should be allocated between the AI and the human.**

The objective is therefore broader than producing a market ranking.

The Case must establish an operational understanding of the Work AI landscape:

```text
Knowledge Work Task
        ↓
Required Capability
        ↓
Work AI Category / Role
        ↓
Candidate Product
        ↓
Product's Actual Work Position
        ↓
Appropriate Usage Pattern
        ↓
Human / AI Work Allocation
        ↓
Potential Multi-AI Handoff
```

### 1.2 Research purpose

The research has four connected purposes:

1. **Market understanding** — understand the major 2026 products and product forms serving Knowledge Work.
2. **Product understanding** — understand what each representative product actually is, what kind of work it is designed to perform, and where it fits in a real workflow.
3. **Task-to-AI understanding** — understand which categories of Knowledge Work are well matched to which AI capabilities and product forms.
4. **Orchestration understanding** — understand how Work AI products can be used independently, sequentially, in parallel, as specialists, as reviewers, or as part of a human-led workflow.

The research should not merely answer:

> “What features does this product have?”

It should primarily answer:

> **“What kind of work does this AI product represent, and how should a knowledge worker think about using it?”**

### 1.3 Non-goal

This Case does not attempt to build a permanent product-to-task mapping, universal AI playbook, or timeless recommendation. Product capabilities, availability, pricing, model access, and interfaces may change after the research snapshot.

## 2. Research Questions

The research must answer the following questions.

### 2.1 Category and taxonomy

1. What is the most useful terminology for this market?
2. How should `Knowledge Work`, `Work AI`, `Work Agent`, and `Office AI` relate to each other?
3. What product forms exist inside the broader Work AI landscape?
4. Where is the boundary between Work AI and adjacent categories such as Coding, Research-only, Search-only, Creation, CRM, or generic Chat?

### 2.2 Market landscape

5. Which products constitute a representative **Global Top 10** for the 2026 Work AI landscape?
6. Which products constitute a representative **China Top 5**, and how does the China landscape relate to the global landscape?
7. What major product categories, strategic directions, and market patterns are visible across the representative set?

### 2.3 Product role and capability

8. What is each representative product's actual work positioning?
9. What types of Knowledge Work tasks is each product designed or observed to handle?
10. What are the product's main work surfaces, context mechanisms, execution capabilities, artifact capabilities, and human interaction patterns?
11. How autonomous is the product in multi-step work, and what remains human-controlled?
12. What differentiates an AI Assistant, Copilot, Agent, and broader Work platform in actual use?

### 2.4 Usage and orchestration

13. How should a knowledge worker frame and hand a task to each major type of Work AI?
14. Which usage patterns are best supported: one-shot interaction, iterative collaboration, artifact-driven work, long-running execution, or human-in-the-loop workflows?
15. What roles can Work AI products play in a larger workflow, such as primary worker, specialist, researcher, synthesizer, operator, reviewer, or coordinator?
16. When does combining multiple AI products materially add capability or reliability, and when is one capable AI sufficient?
17. What recurring orchestration patterns can be observed across the landscape?

### 2.5 Practical decision support

18. Given a task type, what AI capability class should be considered first?
19. What evidence can support choosing among products within the same class?
20. What limitations, access constraints, or uncertainty should a user account for before assigning work to a product?

## 3. Research Scope

### In Scope

The Case covers AI products that materially support **Knowledge Work performed by individuals or teams**, including products whose primary value is one or more of:

- understanding and synthesizing information;
- research and analysis;
- writing and editing;
- document creation and transformation;
- presentations;
- email and communication;
- meetings and meeting artifacts;
- planning and coordination;
- structured knowledge work;
- data-oriented knowledge tasks;
- multi-step task execution;
- persistent work context or project-oriented assistance;
- work-oriented agentic execution;
- work-suite or workspace-level AI assistance.

The scope includes:

- General-purpose AI products when they have substantial Knowledge Work capability.
- Office-suite AI products.
- Work-oriented AI assistants and agents.
- Knowledge-work agents and agentic work products.
- Enterprise-first Work AI products when their capabilities materially shape the Knowledge Work market and are relevant to the research objective.
- Consumer/prosumer products when they are materially used for Knowledge Work.

The scope is global, with a dedicated China market view.

### Out of Scope

The following are not primary research populations unless a product materially overlaps the Knowledge Work problem space:

- Pure foundation models considered only as models.
- AI Coding Agents whose primary purpose is Software Engineering rather than broader Knowledge Work.
- Pure image, video, audio, or creative-generation products.
- Pure customer-service, sales, marketing-automation, or CRM systems without significant general Knowledge Work value.
- Search products that primarily return retrieval results without meaningful Knowledge Work transformation or execution.
- Pure meeting transcription products without substantial downstream Knowledge Work capability.
- Pure workflow automation / RPA infrastructure where AI is not the primary Knowledge Work interface.
- Developer infrastructure, model infrastructure, or research infrastructure.
- Individual product features that do not constitute a meaningful product or product family.

Adjacent products may be discussed when necessary to explain market boundaries, but they do not automatically enter the representative population.

## 4. Research Population

### 4.1 Research unit

The primary research unit is:

> **Product / Product Family**

not:

- Company as a whole;
- Foundation model alone;
- Individual feature;
- Single interface surface.

The preferred abstraction is:

```text
Company
   ↓
Product / Product Family
   ↓
Work Surface(s)
   ↓
AI Capability
   ↓
Work Role
   ↓
Workflow Contribution
```

### 4.2 Candidate universe

A broad candidate universe must be established before the representative set is selected.

The candidate universe should cover, where relevant:

- global Work AI products;
- China Work AI products;
- general-purpose AI with substantial Knowledge Work use;
- Office / productivity-suite AI;
- agentic Knowledge Work products;
- enterprise Work AI;
- AI-native Knowledge Work products;
- important emerging products with clear strategic or workflow significance.

The candidate universe is not itself constrained to 10 or 5 products.

### 4.3 Representative populations

The final landscape contains:

1. **Global Representative Top 10**
2. **China Representative Top 5**

The China Top 5 is a regional cut of the broader landscape and may overlap with the Global Top 10.

The labels `Top 10` and `Top 5` describe a **representative research set**, not a universal claim that product #1 is objectively superior to product #2.

## 5. Definitions

### 5.1 Knowledge Work

> Work in which the primary value creation comes from manipulating, interpreting, creating, organizing, communicating, or deciding with information, concepts, documents, or knowledge.

Examples include research, analysis, writing, planning, document work, presentations, communication, meeting synthesis, knowledge management, and related information-intensive tasks.

### 5.2 AI for Knowledge Work

The overall problem and product space covering AI systems that materially assist or execute Knowledge Work.

### 5.3 Work AI

The preferred short name for the product landscape studied by this Case.

> **Work AI = AI products whose meaningful product value includes assisting or executing Knowledge Work.**

Work AI is a market/category term for this research; it is not intended to imply a single product architecture.

### 5.4 Work Agent

A Work AI product or product mode that can perform a multi-step Knowledge Work objective with meaningful autonomy across context gathering, reasoning, tool use, artifact creation, execution, or verification.

Agentic behavior is defined by what the system actually does, not by whether the vendor uses the word “agent”.

### 5.5 Office AI

A Work AI subset whose primary interaction surface or workflow is office/productivity work such as documents, presentations, spreadsheets, email, meetings, calendars, or office suites.

Office AI is therefore a **sub-category**, not the umbrella term for this Case.

### 5.6 General-purpose AI in this Case

A general-purpose AI product may enter the Work AI population when its Knowledge Work capabilities are substantial enough to be relevant to the Case objective.

The research concerns its **Knowledge Work role**, not its full universe of capabilities.

### 5.7 Product capability vs model capability

The Case distinguishes:

```text
Underlying Model
        ↓
Agent / Product Runtime
        ↓
Context / Memory / Tools / Execution
        ↓
Product Experience
        ↓
Work Capability
        ↓
Workflow Outcome
```

A model benchmark is not automatically evidence of the complete Work AI product capability.

## 6. Inclusion / Exclusion

### Inclusion

A candidate should enter the research population when it satisfies the following principle:

> **It provides material, observable, and relevant capability for Knowledge Work and has sufficient significance or representativeness to contribute to understanding the 2026 Work AI landscape.**

Relevant evidence may include:

- substantial Knowledge Work usage or positioning;
- meaningful multi-step work capability;
- meaningful office/workspace integration;
- meaningful agentic execution;
- strong product adoption or market presence;
- significant enterprise adoption;
- important workflow innovation;
- strong ecosystem significance;
- clear representation of an important category or emerging direction.

### Exclusion

A candidate should be excluded when:

- its main value is outside Knowledge Work;
- it is only a model without a user-facing Work AI product role;
- its work capability is too narrow to materially inform this landscape;
- it duplicates another product family without providing materially distinct Work AI insight;
- reliable evidence is insufficient to characterize it at the required level;
- its inclusion would expand the Case into an adjacent market without improving the actual decision objective.

### Borderline candidates

Borderline cases must be recorded explicitly rather than silently included or excluded.

The decision should state:

- why the boundary was difficult;
- which evidence was used;
- what interpretation was applied;
- what research consequence follows.

## 7. Research Dimensions

The research must analyze products across dimensions that directly support the real-world objective.

### 7.1 Market role

- market presence;
- adoption / usage evidence;
- enterprise or individual relevance;
- ecosystem significance;
- current momentum where credible evidence exists.

### 7.2 Work positioning

Answer:

> **What is this AI actually “for” in a knowledge worker's workflow?**

Examples of possible roles include:

- researcher;
- analyst;
- writer;
- editor;
- planner;
- coordinator;
- document worker;
- meeting assistant;
- knowledge interface;
- operator;
- execution agent;
- reviewer.

These are analytical role labels, not permanent product categories.

### 7.3 Task fit

Analyze which task types the product is materially suited to support or execute, such as:

- research;
- synthesis;
- writing;
- editing;
- document transformation;
- presentation;
- communication;
- meeting work;
- analysis;
- planning;
- structured knowledge work;
- multi-step execution.

Task fit must be supported by product evidence, observed behavior, or credible independent evidence.

### 7.4 Work interaction model

Analyze:

- prompt-driven vs workflow-driven interaction;
- one-shot vs iterative use;
- chat vs workspace vs artifact-centric interaction;
- persistent context;
- project / memory mechanisms;
- human-in-the-loop checkpoints;
- delegation and review patterns.

### 7.5 Agentic execution

Analyze, where applicable:

- planning;
- context gathering;
- tool use;
- browser / external system interaction;
- file manipulation;
- structured artifact creation;
- long-running execution;
- verification;
- retry / repair;
- human approval boundaries.

### 7.6 Artifact model

Analyze what durable work outputs the product can create or transform, such as:

- documents;
- spreadsheets;
- presentations;
- reports;
- research packages;
- structured data;
- task results;
- other durable work artifacts.

### 7.7 Integration and context

Analyze:

- email;
- calendar;
- cloud storage;
- office suites;
- enterprise knowledge;
- web;
- third-party applications;
- connected workspaces;
- identity / permissions / enterprise context where materially relevant.

### 7.8 Usage model

Characterize how a user is expected or observed to use the product:

- direct question-answering;
- iterative co-working;
- delegation;
- background / long-running work;
- artifact review;
- continuous project context;
- specialist invocation;
- verification.

### 7.9 Orchestration role

Analyze the product's possible role in a multi-AI workflow:

```text
Human
  ↕
Primary AI
  ↕
Specialist AI
  ↕
Research / Retrieval AI
  ↕
Creation / Transformation AI
  ↕
Review / Verification AI
```

The research should identify recurring patterns without assuming that multi-AI orchestration is always superior to using one capable AI.

### 7.10 Constraints and economics

Where material to task fit:

- pricing model;
- usage limits;
- access / geography;
- plan tiers;
- enterprise restrictions;
- important workflow constraints;
- notable switching or setup costs.

### 7.11 Evidence and uncertainty

Important claims must distinguish:

- Fact;
- Evidence;
- Analysis;
- Judgment;
- Unknown / unresolved.

## 8. Comparison / Ranking

### 8.1 Comparison objective

The main comparison objective is:

> **Understand how Work AI products differ in work role, task fit, execution model, and workflow contribution.**

The Case should avoid collapsing all differences into a single “best AI” score.

### 8.2 Global Representative Top 10

The Global Top 10 is a representative research set selected from the candidate universe.

Selection should consider the combined evidence of:

- market significance;
- product significance;
- Knowledge Work relevance;
- workflow significance;
- category coverage;
- ability to explain important directions in the 2026 landscape.

No fixed numeric weighting is approved in this Charter.

The final selection must remain evidence-traceable and should explain meaningful inclusion and exclusion decisions.

### 8.3 China Representative Top 5

The China Top 5 uses the same conceptual approach within the China market, while preserving regional context and market differences.

It may overlap with the Global Top 10.

### 8.4 No universal “best” ranking

The research must not present an overall normative ranking such as:

> “Product A is objectively better than Product B for all Knowledge Work.”

Instead, use:

- Representative Top 10 / Top 5;
- capability and task-fit matrices;
- work-role maps;
- workflow archetypes;
- category / role comparisons;
- evidence-backed decision guidance.

Any ordinal presentation used for readability must not be interpreted as a universal quality score.

## 9. Evidence Requirements

### 9.1 Evidence principle

Important conclusions should be traceable:

```text
Claim
↓
Evidence
↓
Source
↓
Date
↓
Confidence / Limitations
```

### 9.2 Source hierarchy

Preferred order:

**Tier 1 — Primary sources**

- official product pages;
- official documentation / help;
- official pricing;
- official release notes / changelogs;
- official blogs / announcements;
- official repositories;
- company reports / filings where applicable.

**Tier 2 — Strong independent evidence**

- credible developer surveys;
- reputable analyst / industry research;
- established technology publications;
- independent benchmark organizations;
- high-quality product testing.

**Tier 3 — Community evidence**

- Reddit;
- Hacker News;
- GitHub Issues / Discussions;
- public user communities;
- other credible practitioner discussions.

**Tier 4 — Individual reviews / blogs**

Useful primarily for product experience, workflow examples, or issue discovery.

### 9.3 Evidence matching

Use evidence appropriate to the claim:

- official sources for product facts and stated capabilities;
- independent evidence for adoption, market impact, and comparative claims where available;
- real user / practitioner evidence for workflow experience and friction;
- public benchmarks only for the layer they actually measure.

### 9.4 Current-state requirement

Product capabilities, prices, plans, integrations, limits, interfaces, and availability are change-sensitive.

For such claims, evidence should be current to the research cutoff whenever reasonably possible.

### 9.5 Uncertainty

The research must preserve:

- conflicting evidence;
- missing data;
- unclear product boundaries;
- vendor claims that lack independent verification;
- differences in measurement methodology.

Do not convert absence of evidence into evidence of absence.

## 12. Success Criteria

The Case is successful when all of the following are true:

### 12.1 Category clarity

A reader can understand why **AI for Knowledge Work / Work AI** is used as the umbrella term and how Work Agent and Office AI relate to it.

### 12.2 Landscape coverage

The research presents an evidence-backed representative Global Top 10 and China Top 5 without pretending that these are universal “best product” rankings.

### 12.3 Product understanding

For each representative product, the research can answer:

> What is it?

> What work is it actually designed to do?

> What role can it play?

> How does a user interact with it?

> What are its important constraints?

### 12.4 Task-to-AI understanding

Given a representative Knowledge Work task, the research provides enough structured information to identify a relevant AI capability class and compare suitable product candidates.

### 12.5 Orchestration understanding

The research explains recurring patterns for:

- one-AI workflows;
- specialist + primary AI workflows;
- AI + human review;
- sequential handoffs;
- parallel comparison / verification.

It should also explain when additional AI does **not** materially improve the workflow.

### 12.6 Evidence quality

Important factual and comparative claims are traceable to appropriate sources, with uncertainty visible where evidence is incomplete or conflicting.

### 12.7 Snapshot integrity

The report accurately reflects the September 24, 2026 research snapshot and does not silently mix later product states into it.

### 12.8 Reusability for AI Work Advisor

The findings should be structured so that a future AI Work Advisor can reason from:

```text
Task
→ Capability
→ Work AI Category / Role
→ Product Candidates
→ Usage Pattern
→ Orchestration Pattern
```

without turning the research itself into a permanent hard-coded product mapping.

### 12.9 Delivery quality

Research Note, PPT, and HTML are mutually consistent, navigable, and sufficiently evidence-backed for practical reuse.

## 13. Open Questions / Assumptions

### 13.1 Assumptions

1. **Work AI** is used as the preferred umbrella term for this Case.
2. **AI for Knowledge Work** describes the broader problem space.
3. **Work Agent** and **Office AI** are treated as sub-categories / product forms within the broader Work AI landscape.
4. General-purpose AI products may enter the population when their Knowledge Work capability is materially relevant.
5. The Global Top 10 and China Top 5 are representative research sets, not universal “best” rankings.
6. China Top 5 may overlap with the Global Top 10.
7. No fixed numeric scoring formula is required by this Charter.
8. Product facts are interpreted as of the September 24, 2026 snapshot unless explicitly stated otherwise.

### 13.2 Open questions to resolve during research

These are research questions, not required pre-execution decisions:

- Which products most clearly establish distinct Work AI archetypes?
- Where should borderline products be placed when vendors and users describe them differently?
- How much of a general-purpose AI product should be considered Work AI when Knowledge Work is only one part of the product?
- Which adoption metrics are comparable enough to support market-significance claims?
- Which observed orchestration patterns are broadly repeatable rather than anecdotal?
- Where do vendor-described agent capabilities differ materially from independently observed behavior?

The research must keep these questions visible until sufficient evidence is available.
